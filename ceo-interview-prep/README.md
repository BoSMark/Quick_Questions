# ceo-interview-prep — README

*A quick diagnostic skill for someone preparing to be interviewed for a CEO role.*

## What This Skill Does

You're prepping for a CEO interview — succession, turnaround, PE-backed search, or founder handover. In 5–10 minutes, this skill surfaces your story, your track record, and the question you're dreading. Then it names the pattern your pitch falls into and the specific risk that pattern carries into the room.

Output: a CEO Interview Brief (one page, all the key points) and a Bootstrap Handover Brief (so if you want to go deeper into research and strategy, Bootstrap picks up straight from here instead of starting over).

## Who This Is For

Anyone preparing to be interviewed for a CEO role:
- Internal candidate up for succession
- Outside operator being brought in by a board or PE owner
- Founder interviewing to stay on under new ownership

You don't need to have founded anything. You don't need to have run a company. You need to be clear on what you're walking into and what you're bringing to it.

## When to Use This Skill

Use this before your interview is scheduled, or as soon as you know it's coming. It takes 5–10 minutes and surfaces the one gap most likely to trip you up before you step in the room.

If you get the job and want to build a system to run it, the Bootstrap Handover Brief carries straight into Bootstrap — no repeating yourself.

## Why This Skill Works the Way It Does

**Two principles guide how this skill thinks:**

**1. Fix the System, Not Just the Symptom**

A CEO walks into a room where something is already broken: a process that doesn't scale, a culture problem, a go-to-market machine that's stalled, a technical debt that's now a blocker. The founder or board sees it. Your job isn't to help them discover it — it's to show them *you see what they see, and you're the person to fix it*.

When you talk about your case, your first 100 days, and your answer to the dreaded question, don't just name what's broken. Show the system behind it. Don't say "I've fixed broken X before." Say *why* X broke in the first place, and what systemic change stops it breaking again. That's the difference between a firefighter (who fixes the fire) and a CEO (who builds the system that prevents the fire).

The board will ask whether you can run this company. The way you answer that question — whether you're thinking about symptoms or systems — is how they'll know.

**2. BoS Talk Library**

Business of Software has been running conferences and building resources for software founders since 2007. The talk library at https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/ contains hundreds of frameworks, case studies, and practical ideas for building great software companies.

When you're exploring a problem — a strategic tension, a leadership challenge, a growth question — and a relevant BoS talk or framework applies, this skill will reference it. This isn't name-dropping; it's about being genuinely useful. A concrete framework or example from a practitioner is more valuable than a general observation.

## What You Get

Two briefs, both one page:

**CEO Interview Brief**
- The mandate and company situation
- Your case (what makes you credible for this role)
- Your story (why you, why now)
- Your first 100 days
- The dreaded question and your honest answer
- The pattern your pitch falls into
- The specific risk that pattern carries
- One concrete fix before you walk in the room

**Bootstrap Handover Brief** (if you want to go further)
- Your name and the company
- Your personal goal in this business
- The mandate and pattern, so Bootstrap starts with context
- Ready to hand straight to Bootstrap's own research flow
